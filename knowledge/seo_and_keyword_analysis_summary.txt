## 2.1 Meta Keywords Reality & True Search Engine Signals
* **Google Reality:** Google has officially IGNORED `<meta name="keywords">` since 2009.
  Adding words solely to the meta keywords array produces zero ranking impact in Google.
* **Bing Nuance:** Bing treats over-stuffed or irrelevant keyword arrays as a potential
  SPAM signal. Safe practice is keeping it strictly to 8-12 relevant terms or omitting it.
* **Where Search Engines Actually Gather Ranking Authority:**
  1. **Page Title Tag (`<title>`):** Highest on-page weight; front-load intent naturally.
  2. **Headings (`<h1>`, `<h2>`, `<h3>`):** Structure topics with clear descriptive hierarchy.
  3. **Natural Body Copy & Interactive FAQs:** Real answers satisfying user intent.
  4. **Clean URLs, Breadcrumbs, & Valid Bidirectional Hreflang:** Architectural clarity.
  5. **Structured Data:** Valid JSON-LD `WebApplication` & `FAQPage` schemas.

## 2.2 The Anti-Boilerplate Rule (August 2026 Core, Spam & Helpful Content Updates)
* **Algorithmic Context & Why Ranks Drop:**
  - Google's Helpful Content System, spam classifiers, and scaled content algorithms
    (strengthened in the August core updates) penalize websites that deploy formulaic,
    templated title tags, headings (`<h1>`, `<h2>`, `<h3>`), and meta descriptions.
  - When multiple pages or language routes share identical syntactic patterns
    (e.g., copying `"[Technique Name] Timer — Guided [Benefit] Breathwork"` uniformly
    across every page, or translating `"[Learn X: Step-by-Step]"` verbatim across all 7 locales),
    Google treats them as programmatic boilerplate or thin duplicated content.
  - **The Direct Consequence:** Rankings collapse into deep sandbox zones (Positions #70–#90+),
    impressions drop, and domain-wide algorithmic trust is degraded.
* **Strict Operational Directives for Titles, Headings & Copy:**
  1. **Independent Title Syntax Across Pages:**
     Every page across HarmonyBreath must utilize an independent grammatical structure,
     distinct punctuation style, and unique semantic angle. Never replicate the same
     sentence structure, colons, hyphens, or formulaic endings across sibling technique pages.
  2. **Authentic Native Phrasing Over 1:1 Translated Templates:**
     Never take a successful English or German heading and translate it directly into
     Spanish, French, or Italian. Each locale must use authentic native phrasing based on
     how real users search in that country:
     - German prefers concise compound terms and action infinitives (*"Bauchatmung lernen"*).
     - French prefers elegant nominal structures (*"Exercices de respiration abdominale et ventrale"*).
     - Spanish prefers action-oriented phrases and natural colloquial variants (*"Ejercicios de respiración..."* or *"Respiración en caja"*).
  3. **Customized Section Subheadings (H2/H3):**
     Every technique page must feature guide headings tailored specifically to that technique's
     physiological mechanism and exact search intent, rather than a generic boilerplate outline.

## 2.3 Strict Per-Language DateModified Isolation Rule
* **Mandate:** NEVER update `dateModified` or visible badges globally across all languages
  when changes are restricted to only 1 or 2 locales.
* Update `dateModified` in JSON-LD and visible badges *strictly* for the language that
  received substantive content/code updates.
* Untouched languages must preserve their original dates to maintain search engine trust
  and prevent false freshness penalties.

## 2.4 Immutable Directive: URL Stability vs On-Page Optimization
* **CRITICAL MANDATE FOR ALL SESSIONS:** NEVER alter, rename, or redirect existing URL slugs,
  routes, or pathnames (e.g., never touch `/box-breathing/`, `/pt/physiological-sigh/`).
* Changing live URLs breaks Google index state, resets accumulated domain trust, triggers
  404 crawl errors, and destroys sandbox progress.
* **The Golden Rule:** The store address (URL) never changes; only update the displays,
  faqs, and content (on-page optimization) inside the store.
