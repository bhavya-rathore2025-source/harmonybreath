What is Nadi Shodhana good for?
AI Overview
Nadi Shodhana, or alternate nostril breathing, is a yogic breath control practice that clears and calms the mind, lowers stress and anxiety, balances the nervous system, and improves lung function by alternating airflow through each nostril. 
What is the difference between Anulom Vilom and Nadi Shodhan?
AI Overview
The main difference between Anulom Vilom and Nadi Shodhan is breath retention (Kumbhaka). Anulom Vilom is a gentle, continuous alternate nostril breathing exercise without holding the breath, making it ideal for beginners. Nadi Shodhan is a more advanced practice that adds controlled breath retention and specific ratios to deeply purify the energy channels.
How many times should I do Nadi Shodhana?
AI Overview
You should practice Nadi Shodhana for 5 to 10 rounds per session if you are a beginner, or for a duration of 5 to 15 minutes if you are tracking by time. Experienced practitioners often scale their practice up to 15 to 20 rounds per session. For optimal, consistent results, this alternate nostril breathing technique should be performed 1 to 2 times daily on an empty stomach
Is Nadi Shodhan safe for everyone?
AI Overview
No, Nadi Shodhana (alternate nostril breathing) is not completely safe for everyone if practiced with breath retention or forced breathing, specifically for individuals with high blood pressure, heart disease, or recent surgeries. While the gentle, basic version without holding the breath is safe for most, certain health conditions require strict caution.
