## Wim Hof Breathing

---

# OVERVIEW

Wim Hof Breathing is a controlled breathing method developed by Wim Hof. It combines rhythmic deep breathing, breath retention after exhalation, and a short recovery breath hold. The technique is commonly practiced for relaxation, mental focus, stress resilience, and increased body awareness.

HarmonyBreath presents this breathing method for educational and wellness purposes only. It does not provide medical advice and should not replace professional healthcare.

---

# HOW A SESSION WORKS

A complete session consists of multiple rounds.

Each round follows this sequence:

### Phase 1 – Guided Breathing

* Take deep, controlled inhales.
* Relax the exhale naturally.
* Continue for **30 breaths**.
* Total guided breathing time is **90 seconds**.

---

### Phase 2 – Breath-Out Hold

* After the final inhale, gently exhale and hold the breath.
* Stay relaxed throughout the hold.
* Never force the hold beyond a comfortable limit.
* Once the target hold duration is reached, the system automatically sounds a chime and transitions to Phase 3.

---

### Phase 3 – Recovery Breath

* Take one deep inhale.
* Hold the inhale for **15 seconds**.
* Exhale naturally.
* Begin the next round.

# BREATHING STYLE

## Inhale

* Deep but comfortable.
* Breathe in deeply in whatever way feels most comfortable and natural to you.

## Exhale

* Relax naturally.
* Do not force all the air out.
* Maintain a smooth, slow rhythm (~2.5s inhale / ~2.5s exhale).

The breathing should always feel relaxed rather than rushed.

# SAFETY

Users should stop the session immediately if they experience:

* Severe dizziness
* Chest pain
* Difficulty breathing
* Loss of consciousness
* Any unusual or concerning symptoms

Never practice Wim Hof Breathing:

* While driving
* While cycling
* While swimming
* During free diving
* While operating machinery
* In any place where losing consciousness could be dangerous

Always practice:

* Sitting comfortably
* Or lying down
* In a safe environment

People who are pregnant or have significant medical conditions should consult a qualified healthcare professional before practicing.

BOX BREATHING STARTS FROM here 

Box breathing, also referred to as square breathing, is a deep breathing technique that can help you slow down your breathing. It works by distracting your mind as you count to four, calming your nervous system, and decreasing stress in your body.

Box breathing is a simple but powerful relaxation technique that can help return your breathing pattern to a relaxed rhythm. It can clear and calm your mind, improving your focus.

Getting Started With Box Breathing
When doing box breathing, it’s good to:

Sit in a chair, stand, or lie down on your back with one hand on your chest and one hand on your stomach. When you sit on a chair, ensure that your back is supported and your feet are firmly on the floor.
Breathe as you would normally for a minute.
Observe the rise and fall of your chest and stomach.
If you notice that your chest is rising but your stomach is not, you are shallow breathing. If your stomach is rising, you are deep breathing, activating full relaxation in your body.
Be aware of your breath to ensure that you are taking deep breaths, allowing your stomach to rise.
If you are lying down or seated on a chair, you will feel your back pressed against the surface when you take a deep breath.
If this is your first time practicing box breathing, push your stomach out while focusing on smooth, deep breaths.
Practice this regularly to encourage deep breathing on a daily basis.

How to do Box Breathing
Box breathing is easy and quick to learn. Anyone can practice this technique and it’s useful in stressful situations when you want to re-center yourself or improve concentration.

Four Steps to Master Box Breathing

Step 1: Breathe in, counting to four slowly. Feel the air enter your lungs.
Step 2: Hold your breath for 4 seconds. Try to avoid inhaling or exhaling for 4 seconds.
Step 3: Slowly exhale through your mouth for 4 seconds.
Step 4: Wait for another 4 seconds before you breathe in again. 
Repeat this exercise until you feel re-centered. Thirty seconds of deep breathing will help you feel more relaxed and in control.

Progression Rules

* All four phases should always remain equal in duration.
* Increase all phases together when progressing.
* Never increase only one phase.
* Users should only move to a longer duration once they can comfortably complete the previous level.
* The technique should always remain calm, smooth, and relaxed.

Custom Session

Allow users to customize:

duration from 2 sec to 10 sec for all phase

The application should validate all custom values and prevent unrealistic or unsafe durations.

Benefits of Box Breathing
Box breathing can be useful in many situations such as:

It can help you cope with panic and stress when feeling overwhelmed. Counting helps to take the focus from the panic-producing situation enabling you to handle and control your response.
Helps you to sleep when you are having insomnia.
Helps to control hyperventilation as you can instruct your lungs to breathe rhythmically.
Helps you refocus when you are having a busy or stressful day.
Eases panic and worry.
Keeps you calm while preparing for the day.
When making a big decision.
Lowers blood pressure and decreases Cortisol — a stress hormone — which can improve your mood.‌
Where to Practice Box Breathing
One benefit of box breathing is that you can practice it anywhere. You can practice it at work, at home, in public, or in a stressful situation. However, you don’t have to practice box breathing only when you are stressed. You can do it to calm your mind and body, allowing yourself to reset and stay fresh.

Tips for Box Breathing
If you're new to this breathing technique, here are some tips:

 If 4 seconds is too much, you can start with 2 or 3 seconds to help you start practicing.
Count the seconds in your head if it is hard to find the beat.
Ensure you are comfortable. It is easier to focus on your breath when you are relaxed and in a comfortable position

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Box Breathing?
A controlled breathing technique with four equal phases that promotes calm and focus.

How long should I practice?
Beginners can start with 3–5 minutes and gradually increase as comfortable.

Can I customize the timing?
Yes. Users can adjust all four phases while maintaining a comfortable rhythm.

Should all four phases be equal?
Yes. Traditional Box Breathing uses equal timing for inhale, both holds, and exhale.

Is Box Breathing safe?
It is generally safe for healthy individuals when practiced gently and without forcing breath holds.

Can beginners use it?
Yes. It is one of the easiest breathing techniques to learn.

4-7-8 Relaxing Breath
What is 4-7-8 Breathing?

The 4-7-8 Relaxing Breath is a calming breathing technique popularized by Dr. Andrew Weil, inspired by traditional yogic breathing practices known as Pranayama. It follows a simple rhythm of inhaling, holding the breath, and exhaling slowly through the mouth. The extended exhale encourages the body to shift from a state of stress into one of deep relaxation.

Unlike energizing breathing methods, the 4-7-8 technique is designed to help quiet the mind, reduce physical tension, and prepare the body for rest.

How It Works

The technique emphasizes a long, controlled exhalation, which naturally activates the parasympathetic nervous system—often called the "rest and digest" system.

When the parasympathetic system becomes more active, your body may respond by:

Slowing the heart rate
Reducing muscle tension
Encouraging a feeling of calm
Lowering mental overactivity
Supporting the transition toward sleep

The breath hold also encourages slower, more mindful breathing and helps break the cycle of rapid, shallow breaths that often accompany stress.

Potential Benefits

People commonly practice the 4-7-8 technique to:

Relax before bedtime
Calm racing thoughts
Reduce everyday stress
Ease feelings of nervousness
Improve focus through slower breathing
Create a mindful pause during a busy day
Support relaxation after work or exercise

Many people notice a calming effect within just a few breathing cycles, although experiences vary from person to person.

Best Time to Practice

The 4-7-8 Relaxing Breath is especially useful when you want to slow down and unwind.

Common times include:

Before going to sleep
After a stressful meeting or exam
During periods of anxiety or overwhelm
Before meditation
During work breaks
While traveling
Before an important presentation
Anytime you need a moment to relax
Who Can Benefit?

This breathing exercise may be suitable for:

Students managing academic stress
Professionals with demanding schedules
People who struggle to relax before bed
Individuals practicing mindfulness
Anyone looking for a simple daily relaxation technique

No previous breathing experience is required.

The Science Behind It

Slow breathing has been widely studied for its effects on the autonomic nervous system.

Research suggests that slower breathing patterns may:

Increase parasympathetic activity
Improve heart rate variability (HRV)
Reduce perceived stress
Promote emotional regulation
Support relaxation and sleep quality

While the exact 4-7-8 pattern itself has limited direct clinical research, many of its principles—particularly slow breathing and extended exhalation—are supported by studies on controlled breathing techniques.

Tips for a Better Experience

For the best results:

Practice in a quiet, comfortable environment.
Keep your shoulders and jaw relaxed.
Breathe gently rather than forcefully.
Stay consistent by practicing daily.
Focus on making the exhale smooth and controlled.
If you feel lightheaded, return to normal breathing and try again later.
Safety

The 4-7-8 Relaxing Breath is generally considered a gentle breathing exercise for healthy adults.

However:

Practice while sitting or lying down when learning.
Stop if you feel dizzy or uncomfortable.
Do not practice while driving, swimming, or operating machinery.
If you have a significant heart condition, respiratory illness, or other medical concerns, consult a healthcare professional before beginning a new breathing practice.
Frequently Asked Questions
Is 4-7-8 breathing good for sleep?

Many people use it before bedtime because the slow breathing rhythm and extended exhale can promote relaxation and help quiet a busy mind.

How often should I practice?

Once or twice a day is a common starting point. Consistency is generally more beneficial than long sessions.

Is it normal to feel lightheaded?

Some beginners may feel slightly lightheaded when first practicing slow breathing. If this happens, stop, breathe normally, and try fewer cycles next time.

Is 4-7-8 breathing the same as box breathing?

No. Box breathing uses equal-length phases, while 4-7-8 places greater emphasis on a longer exhale, making it more focused on relaxation than concentration.

Can beginners try it?

Yes. It is one of the simplest breathing techniques to learn and is often recommended as an introduction to mindful breathing.

Summary

The 4-7-8 Relaxing Breath is a simple yet effective relaxation technique that combines slow inhalation, a comfortable breath hold, and an extended exhalation to encourage calmness. Whether you're winding down after a busy day, preparing for sleep, or simply taking a mindful break, it offers an easy way to slow your breathing and support your body's natural relaxation response.
