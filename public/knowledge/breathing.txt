What is Nadi Shodhana?

Nadi Shodhana, commonly called Alternate Nostril Breathing, is a traditional yogic breathing practice in which you alternate breathing through the left and right nostrils.

The Sanskrit name is often translated as "channel purification" or "channel cleansing." In traditional yoga, nadis are described as subtle energy channels, and the practice is associated with balancing them. Those traditional concepts should be distinguished from modern scientific evidence; there isn't scientific evidence that it literally "cleanses" energy channels.

The modern practice is much simpler:

Inhale through one nostril → exhale through the other → inhale through that nostril → exhale through the opposite nostril → repeat.

Why is it popular?

Nadi Shodhana is one of the most recognizable pranayama practices because it combines:

Slow, controlled breathing
Attention to the breath
Alternation between nostrils
A repetitive rhythm
A simple meditative component

Cleveland Clinic notes that alternate-nostril breathing may help with stress and concentration, and some studies have reported changes in blood pressure and cardiorespiratory measures. However, the strength of evidence varies across outcomes, so it's better to describe these as potential benefits, rather than guaranteed effects.

How it works

The basic sequence is:

Round 1

Left inhale

↓

Right exhale

Round 2

Right inhale

↓

Left exhale

Then repeat.

So one complete cycle is:

Left → Right → Right → Left

More precisely:

Inhale L → Exhale R → Inhale R → Exhale L

Then start again.

Basic technique

Sit comfortably with your spine reasonably upright.

Bring your right hand toward your nose.

A common hand position uses:

Thumb → controls the right nostril
Ring/index finger → controls the left nostril

Then:

Step 1 — Exhale

Take a comfortable exhale.

Step 2 — Left inhale

Close your right nostril with your thumb.

Inhale gently through the left nostril.

Step 3 — Right exhale

Close the left nostril and release the right.

Exhale through the right nostril.

Step 4 — Right inhale

Keep the left nostril closed.

Inhale through the right nostril.

Step 5 — Left exhale

Close the right nostril.

Exhale through the left nostril.

That's one complete cycle.

Cleveland Clinic describes essentially this sequence and suggests practicing for around five minutes as a general wellness practice.

What breathing rate should you use?

There isn't one universally correct Nadi Shodhana timing.

That's another reason I wouldn't hard-code one "scientifically optimal" pattern.

A comfortable beginner pattern could be:

4–4
Inhale: 4 sec
Exhale: 4 sec


Therefore:

Left inhale 4
Right exhale 4
Right inhale 4
Left exhale 4


One complete cycle:

16 seconds

That's:

3.75 complete cycles/minute

or 7.5 individual breaths/minute.

You could also offer a slower option such as:

5–5
Inhale: 5 sec
Exhale: 5 sec


The important principle is comfortable, controlled breathing, rather than forcing a particular number.

Potential benefits
1. Stress reduction

Alternate nostril breathing has been studied as a relaxation-oriented breathing practice. Some studies have reported reductions in perceived stress after regular practice.

2. Concentration

Because the practitioner has to consciously track:

left → right → right → left

the practice naturally requires sustained attention.

Cleveland Clinic describes potential improvements in concentration and alertness.

3. Relaxation

The slow and deliberate breathing pattern can encourage a calmer breathing rhythm.

4. Breathing awareness

The alternating nostril pattern makes you pay close attention to airflow and breathing mechanics.

5. Cardiovascular effects

Some research has reported effects on measures such as blood pressure and cardiorespiratory function, but these findings shouldn't be turned into claims that Nadi Shodhana treats hypertension or cardiovascular disease.

What NOT to claim on HarmonyBreath

I'd avoid statements such as:

❌ "Balances your hormones"

❌ "Detoxifies your body"

❌ "Purifies your energy channels" as a scientific fact

❌ "Activates the parasympathetic nervous system" as an absolute guarantee

❌ "Lowers blood pressure" as a guaranteed medical effect

❌ "Improves brain function"

Instead:

"Nadi Shodhana is traditionally practiced to promote balance and calm. Modern research suggests it may help reduce perceived stress and support relaxation and concentration."

That's much more defensible.

When to practice it

Nadi Shodhana works particularly well as a:

🧘 Meditation preparation

A few minutes before meditation.

🧠 Focus practice

Before studying or working when you want a structured breathing exercise.

🌿 Relaxation practice

After a stressful activity or during a quiet break.

🌙 Evening practice

It can be used in the evening, although individual responses differ.

Safety

For a general wellness timer, keep the instructions conservative.

Practice seated or in another safe position, and don't use breathwork while driving, swimming, bathing, or doing anything where dizziness or distraction could be dangerous.

Stop if you experience:

dizziness
faintness
chest pain
significant shortness of breath
unusual discomfort

And don't force the breath.

This is especially important if you add breath retention presets, because holding the breath isn't necessary for beginners.
